Methods

Two tiers, one rule: every value on this site points back to a record in a public archive or an open paper. The registry classifies every ENA study with a human shotgun-metagenome signal from its archive record. The catalog curates the human-gut studies of the registry, all ages, at sample level: four routes recover values, each committed only with a verbatim evidence quote (≤ 12 words), a labelled source, a locator, a route and a confidence; unknown stays unknown. Every release is immutable and versioned (Releases); code, configuration and vocabularies live in catalog-pipeline.

1 · Registry: enumeration and classification

Every ENA study with METAGENOMIC / WGS-type runs and a human signal (host taxon 9606, human in the sample or study text, or a human body-site term) is enumerated from the ENA portal API in dated slices (registry_universe_audit.csv proves completeness per slice). Per study the questions are: host (human / non-human / mixed / unknown), body sites from a controlled vocabulary anchored to UBERON (17 terms), life stages, population flags, assay (shotgun / amplicon / genome / transcriptome / other) and access. Deterministic rules decide what the archive fields settle (host taxon, library fields, numeric age with unit); the rest is classified by two independent model replicates over title, description and sample attributes, disagreements adjudicated by a stronger model; every call carries its evidence rows. 54,367 of 54,410 studies are classified (100 %); 43 are pending. The sample tier harvests every BioSample of the human studies and normalises site, age, sex and country from its attributes (611,601 BioSamples).

2 · Catalog scope

A registry study enters the catalog when its host is human, its assay is shotgun and gut / stool is among its body sites (Scope shows the funnel and what is left out). Every harvested BioSample of such a study is a catalog sample; its body_site_class says whether it is a gut sample (primary), has no site attribute (unknown) or belongs to another site of the same study (excluded / linked). Age categories (neonate, infant, child, adolescent, adult, elderly) come from the numeric age when known, else from the sample's stated life stage, a cohort-wide statement, or the study's primary life stage — the basis is recorded per sample.

4 · Evidence and confidence

Every row of gut_sample_determinations.parquet carries evidence_source (e.g. sample.attr.host_age, paper.supplement.<file>, paper.fulltext.methods, paper.abstract), evidence_locator (attribute key, PMCID, PMID or accession), evidence_quote (≤ 12 words, verbatim), determined_by (parser or model role and version) and a confidence tier. Confidence is an engine tier, not a calibrated probability (Fields › Confidence). Model ids are resolved per role at run time from config/models.yaml; the exact model is recorded on every model-derived row.

5 · Cohorts, papers, authors

Papers are linked to studies from Europe PMC (accession mentions, data-availability statements) and NCBI BioProject publication records, with a relation (own_data when the paper describes the deposit, unsure when it may re-analyse it). Authors are the Europe PMC author lists of those papers, string-matched and not disambiguated. Cohorts group studies that share an own-data paper or a curated cohort record (connected components); the grouping is recomputed at every release.

6 · Releases and provenance

Releases are immutable and numbered (R2026.n); each maps to a data-package semver and to Git tags in the data and site repositories. Fact tables carry release_added / release_retired / package_added; the changes pages list what each release added. The site is generated deterministically from the package (byte-identical rebuilds); data/manifest.json lists every data file with its SHA-256.

7 · Limitations

  • Coverage is bounded by what submitters deposited and what open papers state: 26 % of samples have an age, 37 % a health-condition code, 31 % a sex, 16 % an antibiotic-exposure value.
  • Case–control cohorts stay unknown at sample level unless an attribute or table names the group; cohort-wide codes are assigned only when every sequenced subject shares the condition.
  • Archive ages without a unit are read as years for non-infant studies; ambiguous or conflicting values are not committed.
  • R2 reads only machine-readable supplements (xlsx / csv) that join on exact identifiers; R3 reads one paper per study, with Methods truncated for a minority of studies; no full-text route has been run for studies without an open-access paper.
  • Registry classification of pending studies and the model-based calls carry their evidence but are not human-reviewed; use the Flag an issue buttons.
  • Sandpiper community profiles are shown where they exist (currently the infant studies' runs); the registry records how many runs of each study are in the Sandpiper snapshot.
