Archive description

Preterm birth is a major global cause of neurodevelopmental impairment (NDI), yet early predictive biomarkers are lacking. The gut microbiome, which develops in parallel with the brain and communicates via the microbiota-gut-brain axis, may offer early indicators of NDI risk. This project investigates the longitudinal trajectories of the gut microbiome and metabolome in preterm infants stratified by neurodevelopmental risk.</p><p>We conducted a prospective, matched, longitudinal study of 60 preterm infants (born <37 weeks of gestation). At 3 months corrected age, infants were classified into high-risk (HR, n=30) or low-risk (LR, n=30) groups for NDI based on standardized motor (Test of Infant Motor Performance) and neurological (General Movements Assessment) evaluations. Groups were rigorously matched for gestational age, birth weight, sex, and clinical exposures to minimize confounding.</p><p>Fecal samples were collected at two time points: meconium and at 3 months corrected age. Shotgun metagenomic sequencing (this dataset) and untargeted metabolomics (LC-MS) were performed to characterize the microbial taxonomic composition, functional potential, and metabolic profiles.</p><p>The data reveal that while overall microbial community structure (alpha- and beta-diversity) does not differ between risk groups, profound taxonomic and functional divergence emerges by 3 months. The LR gut is enriched with beneficial symbionts such as Akkermansia muciniphila, whereas the HR gut is dominated by pathobionts like Klebsiella variicola. Functional metagenomics indicates that the HR microbiome is enriched for pathways related to bacterial virulence, stress response, and inflammation, contrasting with the LR microbiome which expands core biosynthesis functions. Critically, differences in meconium microbiota correlate with later neurobehavioral scores, suggesting ultra-early associative potential.</p><p>This dataset provides resource for understanding the role of the gut microbiome in early neurodevelopment and offers potential multi-omics biomarkers for early risk stratification. All raw metagenomic sequences (FASTQ files) and associated metadata are made publicly available to facilitate further research into microbiota-targeted interventions for improving neurodevelopmental outcomes in preterm infants.
