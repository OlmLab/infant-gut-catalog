Archive description

BackgroundOne of the strongest signals of human genetic effects on the gut microbiome is the association between the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium and host genotype at the locus for the lactase gene (LCT), which is associated with lactase persistence. In several populations of European descent, individuals with lactase non-persistence have been shown to harbor a greater proportion of Bifidobacterium in stool compared to those with lactase persistence. These associations are based on 16S rRNA gene data that do not discriminate between species of Bifidobacterium. Given the wide functional and taxonomic diversity of this genus, a species-level resolution of the LCT-Bifidobacterium association would allow better understanding of the underlying relationship. Here, we further interrogated the LCT-Bifidobacterium in adult monozygotic twins that were concordant for either lactase persistence (AA/AG) or non-persistence (GG). We used metagenomics on (1) bulk DNA extracts and (2) post genome-capture with a Bifidobacterium-specific probe array.ResultsWe observed specific Bifidobacteria are proportionally enriched in GG versus AG/AA individuals, which are both dominated by B. adolescentis and B. longum. We found no discriminant Bifidobacterium species between GG and AG/AA genotypes, nor any genotypic signal on Bifidobacterium community structure. Our genome capture data revealed strong intra-individual longitudinal stability of B. longum, B. adolescentis, and B. bifidum strains, and increased intra-twin strain similarities compared to unrelated individuals, but did not reveal an effect of host lactase persistence genotype on strain stability or strain composition. ConclusionsThe Bifidobacteria are often considered specialists in human gut communities. Yet our study suggests a wide niche space across all human associated Bifidobacteria, which would enable them to exploit the greater availability of intact lactose reaching the large intestine of lactase non-persistent people.
