Effects of Probiotic and Dietary Interventions on Gut Microbiota and Weight Loss in Obese Children

Archive description

In recent years, childhood obesity has become a major global public health challenge, with its pathogenesis closely linked to gut microbial dysbiosis and metabolic disorders. This two-phase study aimed to characterise the faecal metagenomic profiles of obese children and investigate the beneficial effects of Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis ProSci-246 on obese school-age children. The first-phase cohort revealed significant differences in gut microbial composition and carbohydrate function between obese and healthy children. One hundred and twelve obese children who met the inclusion criteria were randomly assigned to either a probiotic group or a placebo group for a three-month intervention, alongside dietary guidance. A total of 95 children (n = 44 in the probiotic group and n = 51 in the placebo group) provided BMI measurements, blood biochemistry panels and stool samples. Following the intervention, BMI and blood uric acid levels decreased significantly in the probiotic group compared to the placebo group. Furthermore, beneficial bacteria such as Bifidobacterium animalis and Lactiplantibacillus plantarum increased, while obesity-associated species such as Faecalibacterium duncaniae and Gemmiger sp900540595 decreased significantly. Fecal metabolomics revealed modulation of energy metabolism, bile acid metabolism and the urea cycle. This study showed that ProSci-246 probiotics may have additional effects in the treatment of obese school-age children by regulating intestinal flora, altering the breakdown and utilisation of polysaccharides and adjusting energy metabolism. This provides a promising, microbiome-based strategy for managing childhood obesity and related metabolic complications.
